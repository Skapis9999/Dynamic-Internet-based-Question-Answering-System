This process occurs in two main stages: the light-dependent reactions and the light-independent reactions (Calvin cycle) [1]. 
>

> 
> The light-independent reactions, or Calvin cycle, take place in the stroma of the chloroplasts. Here, carbon dioxide from the atmosphere is fixed into organic molecules, such as glucose, using the ATP and NADPH generated in the light-dependent reactions [1][2]. 
> 
> Photosynthesis is the reverse of cellular respiration, where carbohydrates are broken down to release energy [1]. The overall equation for photosynthesis is: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ [3].
